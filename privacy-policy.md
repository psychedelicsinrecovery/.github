<a id="promise"></a>
## 1. Our promise, rooted in anonymity

Anonymity is the spiritual foundation of our traditions. Taking part in PIR can involve sharing
that you're in recovery, or that you're curious about psychedelics as part of your path, and we
know how personal that is. Spiritually, anonymity isn't secrecy. It's the reminder that no one is greater or less than anyone
else. In a world of accounts, databases, and recording devices, we honor it by respecting everyone in
our shared atmosphere. So we work from three commitments:

- **We collect as little as we can,** and only for a clear service reason.
- **We never sell your information,** never rent it, and never use it to advertise to you on
  other sites.
- **We tell you plainly** what our websites and tools do, including the less glamorous parts.

<a id="collect"></a>
## 3. What we collect, feature by feature

### Browsing our websites
- Our hosting provider (WPX) and security tools record standard technical information such as IP
  address, browser and pages requested. They use it to keep the sites running and safe from
  attacks.
- **WP Statistics** counts visits on our own server, with **IP addresses anonymized and hashed**,
  so we can't identify you from them.
- **Google Analytics** (via Google Site Kit) also measures site usage. See [Cookies](#cookies).

### Meeting notifications & newsletter signups
- Our meeting-notification signup has asked for your **first name, last initial and email**, and
  for two optional self-descriptions: that you have *a desire to recover*, and/or *an interest in
  psychedelics as an aid to transformation*. We know those statements are sensitive. We use them
  only to send meeting information, never share them, and are setting a retention limit (see
  [How long we keep things](#retention)).
- Newsletter and general signup forms collect your name and email. Signups are stored on our
  website and passed to our email provider (**Brevo**; previously **Mailchimp** and **MailPoet**)
  so we can send you mail. Every email has an unsubscribe link.

### Contact & committee forms
Contact forms on both sites (and service-site forms such as meeting-room registration or surveys)
collect what you enter, typically your name, email and message. We use it only to respond, or for
the purpose the form describes.

### Comments
If you leave a comment, we collect what's in the form plus your IP address and browser details,
for spam detection (**Akismet**, by Automattic). An anonymized string made from your email (a
"hash") may be checked with **Gravatar** to show your profile picture.

### Convention passes & checkout (WooCommerce)
- When you buy a convention pass, we collect the order details needed to deliver it and to meet
  financial record-keeping requirements: name, email, billing address and order contents.
  **Payments are processed by PayPal or WooPayments.** We never see or store your full card
  number.
- **If you start checkout but don't finish**, our cart-recovery tool saves the billing details
  you've typed, so we can send a gentle reminder or help if something went wrong. If you'd rather
  we didn't keep those details, ask, and we'll delete them by hand. (It's a good example of a
  deletion request; see [Your choices](#rights).)
- Checkout may offer an optional box to receive PIR emails. It's unticked unless you choose it.

### Donations (our 7th Tradition)
- Donations go through **PayPal**. PayPal shares the donation details with us so we can keep
  records and thank you.
- In keeping with Tradition 7, PIR is self-supporting and **declines outside contributions**.

### Online meetings (Zoom)
Unless you join with camera or audio, other participants see only your display name (an alias is
fine). PIR's Zoom hosts can see the email address or phone number you used to join. We use them only
to run the meeting and never share them.

### Website accounts (service members and editors)
If you have a login on either website (for example, as a committee editor), we store your username,
email, and profile details. You can view and edit them any time, except your username. Password-reset
emails include the IP address the request came from, so you can spot a request you didn't make.

### Media uploads
If you upload images, please remove embedded location data (EXIF GPS) first. Anyone who downloads
an image can read it.

### Embedded content
- Some pages embed content from other services, such as our **meetings calendar (Google
  Calendar)**, YouTube videos and PDF viewers.
- Embedded content behaves as if you visited that service directly, which means the service may
  set its own cookies or learn that you viewed the page.

<a id="cookies"></a>
## 4. Cookies and analytics

- **Essential cookies:** logging in (2 days; 2 weeks with "Remember Me"), screen options (1
  year), comment-form convenience (1 year, only if you opt in), and checkout and cart sessions.
- **Google Analytics (GA4) and Google Tag Manager** on the main site, and GA4 on the service site.
  These help us understand which pages help people. Logged-in editors are excluded. Google acts
  under its own [privacy policy](https://policies.google.com/privacy).
- **Jetpack (Automattic):** site statistics, security (firewall and brute-force protection), and
  email subscriptions.
- **Brevo:** our email provider may use a small tracking script to understand which pages
  subscribers visit, plus **optional browser push notifications** that you must explicitly allow.
- **No social advertising pixels are connected.** Some store plugins that *could* connect to ad
  platforms are installed but unused. **We'll update this policy before connecting any of them.**

You can block or delete cookies in your browser at any time. The site will still work, though
checkout needs session cookies.

## 5. How we use AI and helper tools

PIR is run by volunteers. To make our small amount of service time go further, some of our
trusted service members use **AI assistants** to help with the behind-the-scenes work: keeping the
websites tidy, drafting documents like this one, organizing committee tasks and watching shared
inboxes so nothing falls through the cracks. Here's how we keep that respectful:

- **The helpers work for service members, not on you.**
  - We use **Claude** (by Anthropic) as a coding and writing assistant.
  - We use **Littlebird** as an organizational assistant that keeps track of our work, observes
    the screen of the service member who uses it, and prepares a daily summary of some PIR role
    inboxes for the service member who monitors them.
- **What they can see:**
  - website content and settings;
  - documents in our GitHub organization;
  - and, for Littlebird, the role mailboxes it's asked to summarize, such as `board@` or
    `convention@`. If you email one of those addresses, an AI assistant may help a service
    member read and sort your message.
- **What we don't do:**
  - we don't deliberately feed member lists or personal shares into AI tools;
  - we don't use AI to make decisions about you;
  - we don't build profiles of anyone.
- **Training:**
  - Littlebird states that it doesn't use personal data to train AI models, deletes data when an
    account is closed, and holds zero-data-retention agreements with the AI providers it uses.
  - Anthropic lets account holders choose whether their conversations help train Claude. PIR asks
    service members to turn training settings off wherever a tool offers that choice.
- 🎙️ **AI note-takers and personal assistants are part of the world now.** Service members may use
  transcription and assistant tools such as **Fathom, Otter.ai, Fireflies.ai, Notion, Zoom AI
  Companion, Wispr Flow, Parakeet, FreeFlow, Meetily,** and **Littlebird**. Some of these observe a
  person's own screen, so they can capture whatever that person sees, including a Zoom or WhatsApp
  window. These tools run on *individual members'* devices and accounts. PIR doesn't operate them and
  can't technically control them.
  - **Service meetings** (committee, board, and business meetings) may be transcribed to help draft
    minutes. Members who transcribe are asked to say so. Most service members dispose of transcripts
    once the minutes are written, but each tool keeps data under its own settings.
  - **Recovery meetings should never be recorded or transcribed.** Many meetings say so explicitly,
    and some read a script intro reminding newcomers that, in a virtual room, others may be "watching
    with more than human eyes."
  - Like recording a virtual court hearing, capturing other people's shares without consent breaks
    trust. In our community it's held together by **mutual respect, not a technical gate**. Please
    honor it.
- **Access is limited** to specific service members and revocable at any time, and every change
  our helpers make to the websites is logged and can be rolled back.

We think these tools are a gift to a volunteer fellowship, and we use them with the same care
we'd want for our own information.

<a id="discord"></a>
## 6. Discord, A.L.E.X. and the helpdesks

PIR's Discord has a helper called **A.L.E.X.** (*Astral Logic Endpoint eXpander*). It answers
questions using PIR's public website and published literature, and runs our LitCom, TechCom,
GitHub and moderation helpdesks.

- **A.L.E.X. can't read your chats.** It only sees what you type into its own slash commands and
  forms, such as `/ask` or `/techcom`.
- **When you `/ask`:**
  - your question goes to an AI model to write the answer;
  - during our pilot that's an NVIDIA-hosted model, and later possibly Anthropic's Claude;
  - **we send only the question text, never your name or Discord ID;**
  - we keep only anonymous counts (for example "a question was asked", or "👍").
- **If something sounds like a crisis,** A.L.E.X. skips the AI entirely and shows crisis
  resources right away. It may also alert a human support role.
- **When you open a helpdesk ticket:**
  - your request goes into a private Discord thread that only you and the relevant committee can
    see;
  - LitCom, TechCom and GitHub tickets also create an entry in that committee's **private**
    GitHub repository, so the work can be tracked;
  - moderation reports stay inside Discord.
- **Web helpdesk sign-in** uses either your Discord account (we only receive your Discord ID and
  username) or a **one-time email link**, with no password. We keep your email only to send
  updates on your ticket.
- Discord itself operates under [Discord's privacy policy](https://discord.com/privacy).

The full step-by-step data flow for A.L.E.X. and the desks is documented in the A.L.E.X. privacy
notice (`psychedelicsinrecovery/alex-desk`, published with the helpdesk portals).

statistics from Google Analytics, Jetpack, and Brevo; and spam verdicts from Akismet.

<a id="retention"></a>
## 8. How long we keep things

- **Orders:** as long as financial record-keeping rules require.
- **Abandoned-checkout details:** until they're no longer useful for helping you finish (we're
  setting an automatic clean-up), or immediately on request.
- **Older form signups** (e.g. past meeting-notification lists): kept while PIR migrates its tools, and
  **deleted on request**. Email **tech@psychedelicsinrecovery.org**.
- **Email lists:** until you unsubscribe.
- **Comments:** kept indefinitely, so we can recognize returning commenters, unless you ask us to
  remove them.
- **Helpdesk tickets:** kept while useful to the committee, then archived. Ask us any time to
  delete yours.
- **A.L.E.X. questions:** not stored.
- **Service-meeting transcripts:** held in individual members' tools under those tools' settings; most are
  disposed of once minutes are written. Approved minutes are kept as PIR's official records, as our bylaws require.

<a id="rights"></a>
## 9. Your choices and rights

You can ask us to **show you, correct, export or delete** what we hold about you, except what
we're legally required to keep. Unsubscribe links are in every email. Email **helpdesk@psychedelicsinrecovery.org** (or **tech@psychedelicsinrecovery.org** for data deletion) (or use `/privacy` in our Discord). A real person will
reply, usually within a week.

<a id="security"></a>
## 10. Keeping data safe, and what happens if something goes wrong

- Our sites use HTTPS, a firewall, brute-force protection, regular backups and least-privilege
  access for helpers and service members.
- Credentials are held by a small number of trusted servants.
- If we learn of a breach affecting your information, we'll tell affected people and our board
  promptly, explain what happened and what we're doing about it, and notify authorities where the
  law requires.

<a id="automated"></a>
## 11. Automated decisions and profiling

We don't make decisions about people with automated systems, and we don't build profiles of anyone.
The closest thing is A.L.E.X.'s safety check, which only decides *which resources to show*: crisis
resources, or a kind note that we can't give dosing or medical advice. It never stores or reports
what you wrote.

<a id="legal"></a>
## 12. Legal and regulatory notes

- PIR is a peer-support fellowship and nonprofit, **not a healthcare provider**. We don't provide
  medical, clinical, or crisis services, and we aren't a HIPAA covered entity. Please don't send us
  medical records.
- Card payments are handled entirely by our payment processors under their own security standards
  (PCI DSS). We never store card numbers.
- 🌍 PIR's members live all over the world: the US, UK, EU, Switzerland, New Zealand, Costa Rica, and
  beyond. Whatever privacy law applies where you live, you have the rights in [§9](#rights), and we honor
  the same requests for everyone.
